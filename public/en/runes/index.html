📜 Brief Historical Overview of the Elder Futhark

The Elder Futhark is the oldest runic system, formed approximately in the 2nd–3rd centuries CE in Northern Europe among Germanic tribes. It consists of 24 runes and was used both for everyday purposes (inscriptions, markings) and in sacred practice.

The name “Futhark” comes from the first six runes: Fehu, Uruz, Thurisaz, Ansuz, Raidho, Kenaz.

Runes were carved on stone, wood, metal, weapons, and everyday objects. However, it is important to understand: they were not merely letters. Even in the earliest findings, runes already carry symbolic and magical meaning.

Over time, the Elder Futhark evolved into other systems (Younger Futhark, Anglo-Saxon runes), but it is considered the foundational and most “pure” form of the runic tradition.

🧿 Brief Mythological Description

According to the Northern tradition, runes were not invented by humans.

They were revealed through Odin’s sacrifice. For nine days and nights, he hung on the World Tree Yggdrasil, pierced by a spear, without food or water, suspended between life and death. In this state, he “saw” the runes — not as symbols, but as forces underlying the fabric of reality.

Thus, runes are not an alphabet in the conventional sense. They are:

  • principles of reality
  • energetic structures
  • archetypal forces

Each rune is not a sign, but a living formula reflecting a specific aspect of existence: movement, destruction, growth, death, cycle, flow, etc.

⚡ Principle of Understanding Runes

This page, like any “grimoire” or “description,” is only an external map.

True understanding of runes comes not through reading, but through:

  • lived experience
  • interaction
  • personal practice

A rune cannot be fully understood logically. It can only be:

  • felt
  • internalized
  • allowed to manifest in consciousness and body

🜂 Important Guidance for the Practitioner

Each rune must be studied not only intellectually, but also through the “soul” — via one’s own energy.

This means:

  • observing the rune and inner sensations
  • embodying its state (e.g., Isa as stillness, Uruz as strength)
  • working with breath and body
  • tracking how the rune manifests in reality

Any text, even the most detailed one, is only a starting point.

Real knowledge comes when:

  • the rune begins to “respond”
  • its state becomes recognizable within
  • its power manifests in practice

The Elder Futhark is not just a symbolic system.

It is a tool for:

  • self-knowledge
  • interaction with the world
  • transformation of reality

But only under one condition:

if the practitioner goes beyond the text and begins to work with runes as living forces.
